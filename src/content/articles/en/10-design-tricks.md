## 1. Light Color Palette

Light colors make spaces feel larger. White, beige, and light gray form the base, paired with wood tones for warmth.

## 2. Remove Non-Load-Bearing Walls

Open-plan living (LDK — Living, Dining, Kitchen) is the #1 strategy for making small homes feel big.

## 3. Unified Flooring

Same flooring throughout the main areas creates visual continuity and makes the space feel larger.

## 4. Mirrors Are Magic

A large mirror on a hallway wall or living room side wall instantly doubles visual space.

## 5. Low-Profile Furniture

Low sofas, beds, and coffee tables keep eye focus low, making ceilings appear higher.

## 6. Floor-to-Ceiling Curtains

Mount curtain rods as close to the ceiling as possible. Floor-length drapes beat half-window curtains every time.

## 7. Vertical Lines

Vertical wall stripes, full-height cabinet doors, and linear pendant lights visually stretch the room upward.

## 8. Hidden Storage

Clutter is enemy #1 in small spaces. Full-wall cabinets, built-ins, and tatami platforms keep things invisible.

## 9. Multi-Function Furniture

Folding tables, sofa beds, and transformable furniture serve multiple purposes and save space.

## 10. Intentional Empty Space

Not every wall needs something on it. Empty space lets the room breathe and feels more open.
